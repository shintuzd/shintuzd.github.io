About the data

Source

  • City Employee Payroll, published by the Los Angeles City Controller. It covers every City employee, including the three proprietary departments: Water and Power (LADWP), Airports and Harbor. Downloaded .
  • LA residents: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2024 1-year estimates for the City of Los Angeles (tables B03002 and B01001). City employees don't all live in the city, and residents include children and retirees, so this is a rough benchmark, not a target.

Who is counted

  • The payroll file has a row for every job a person held in a year, so a transfer or second job adds rows. Rows are combined into one record per person per year using the employee record number. The person's job and department are the ones with the most regular pay.
  • Full-time employees: anyone with a full-time pay record during the year. Part-time and per-event workers are left out.
  • Full-year, full-time workers: full-time employees who were also on the payroll the year before and the year after, so they were employed the whole year. All pay comparisons use this group, which keeps new hires and people who left partway through from pulling medians down.
  • Total pay is regular pay plus overtime plus other pay (bonuses, assignment pay, leave payouts and similar). It does not include benefits or the City's retirement contributions. Names are never used or published.

How the same-job gap is measured

  • For each year, the log of total pay is regressed on gender and race/ethnicity, with a separate baseline for every job classification. The result compares women with men, and each group with White employees, who hold the same job.
  • A job classification is the City's class code, so Police Officer I, II and III count as one job (the level within a class is part of what's compared). LADWP classifications are kept apart from the rest of the City because LADWP has its own pay scales.
  • This doesn't account for seniority, hours or which shifts and assignments people get. A gap in the same job can come from overtime, assignment pay or reaching higher levels within a class. Estimates resting on fewer than 30 people who share a job with the comparison group are left out.

Data issues

  • LADWP 2025: LADWP's payroll is posted on a slower schedule, and only about half of its 2025 pay is in the file so far. 2025 pay figures therefore leave LADWP out (headcounts still include it). In 2018–2024, leaving LADWP out moved the citywide gender gap by at most percentage points.
  • Race/ethnicity labels changed in 2024 (“Caucasian” became “White”, most “Filipino” records became “Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander”, and new categories appeared). Groups are combined so they line up across years: Asian, Asian American, Filipino and Pacific Islander are shown together.
  • Missing demographics: many 2025 records, mostly separated employees and payroll adjustments, have no gender or race/ethnicity. Where the same employee has it recorded in another year, that value is used. Records still unknown (about 5% of full-time employees for race/ethnicity) are left out of shares.
  • The payroll records gender as male or female, with a few dozen “other” records a year, so it can't show nonbinary employees.
  • 2025–2026 records moved into the City's new Workday system without a job classification (“XXXX”) are left out of the same-job comparisons.
